\begin{cabstract}
合成孔径雷达（Synthetic Aperture Radar, SAR）凭借其全天时、全天候的主动成像能力，
已成为军事侦察与战场态势感知的关键手段。
受SAR成像机理影响，图像中普遍存在相干斑噪声，且目标在不同传感器参数、
观测角度与成像条件下呈现显著的特征差异，
使得传统人工判读方式难以满足复杂场景下快速、准确、自动化识别的应用需求。
近年来，基于深度学习的SAR自动目标识别技术取得了显著进展，但在实际应用中仍面临三大核心挑战：
实测训练数据获取困难、跨域数据间分布偏移显著、以及边缘平台部署资源受限。

本文面向目标域实测数据受限条件下的跨域SAR目标识别需求，
针对“目标域数据少样本”和“目标域数据不可见”两种典型场景，
分别研究域适应和域泛化SAR目标识别方法，并进一步实现模型在国产AI芯片平台上的轻量化部署与验证。
其中，域适应问题旨在利用可访问的少量目标域数据缩小源域与目标域之间的分布差异；
域泛化问题则侧重于挖掘源域中的域不变信息，以提升模型对不可见目标域的泛化能力。
主要研究内容与贡献如下：

（1）提出了一种基于像素级域对齐和类混淆损失的少目标域样本域适应SAR目标识别方法。
针对现有的SAR图像无监督域适应方法过度依赖大量目标域数据的问题，
在像素级上引入基于对比学习的非配对图像翻译模型
替代传统的循环生成对抗模型（CycleGAN），
利用块噪声对比估计损失实现少样本条件下的高质量像素级域对齐；
在特征级上设计类混淆损失与随机数据增强一致性正则化机制，
通过概率校准与不确定性加权直接优化目标域判别边界，
降低对目标域数据完整统计分布的依赖。
基于SAMPLE数据集的无监督域适应实验结果显示，在跨域SAR目标识别任务中，
所提方法在目标域样本极少的条件下仍能保持较高的识别性能，
表明该方法能够在少样本条件下有效完成跨域对齐与判别边界优化，
大幅降低了跨域SAR目标识别模型对目标域数据量的依赖。

（2）提出了一种基于多层级数据增强和对抗学习的域泛化SAR目标识别方法。
针对目标域数据完全不可见的极端场景，
设计了像素级数据增强、特征级风格混淆和域对抗学习三大模块协同工作的域泛化识别框架。
像素级模块基于SAR成像特性设计高斯噪声注入、高频频域扰动和分贝化变换，扩展源域数据分布多样性；
特征级模块基于实例归一化设计域风格混淆机制，通过混合实例级特征统计量，在深层特征空间模拟多样化域风格；
域对抗学习模块利用梯度反转层构建极小极大博弈，剥离域特异性信息以学习域不变特征。
在SAMPLE和S2M-5两个数据集上的域泛化实验结果显示，在跨域SAR目标识别任务中，
所提方法在不同数据集上均表现出良好的识别性能和跨域稳定性，
表明该方法能够在目标域不可见条件下有效提升模型对未知实测域的泛化能力与鲁棒性，
为“开放世界”中未知域偏移带来的性能衰退提供了应对策略。

（3）实现了面向国产AI芯片的跨域SAR目标识别模型轻量化部署。
以第二章域适应方法和第三章域泛化方法训练所得的两个蕴含跨域信息的ResNet50权重为基础，
设计并验证了“ResNet50教师网络—MobileNetV2学生网络”的知识蒸馏框架，
通过软硬标签联合监督将两类跨域判别知识迁移至轻量级结构，在显著降低模型参数规模的同时有效保留跨域识别能力；
基于华为昇腾模型压缩工具AMCT设计INT8后训练均匀量化方案，
采用权重逐通道对称量化与激活IFMR非对称校准的混合策略，进一步压缩模型体积并降低推理时延；
依托华为Atlas 200I DK A2开发者套件构建从模型导出、AMCT量化、ATC转换到AscendCL板端推理的完整部署流程。
部署实验表明，所提方法在显著压缩模型规模、降低存储开销和推理时延的同时，仍能较好保持模型跨域识别性能，
打通了“跨域识别模型训练—轻量化压缩—国产化端侧部署”的完整技术链路。


\end{cabstract}

\begin{eabstract}
Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), by virtue of its all-time and all-weather active imaging capability, has become a key means for military reconnaissance and battlefield situational awareness. Owing to the SAR imaging mechanism, SAR images are generally contaminated by speckle noise, and targets exhibit significant feature variations under different sensor parameters, viewing angles, and imaging conditions, making traditional manual interpretation inadequate for the demands of rapid, accurate, and automated recognition in complex scenarios. In recent years, deep learning-based SAR automatic target recognition (ATR) has achieved remarkable progress. However, practical applications still face three core challenges: the difficulty of acquiring measured training data, significant distribution shifts across domains, and limited deployment resources on edge platforms.

This thesis addresses the need for cross-domain SAR target recognition under the condition of limited measured data in the target domain. For two typical scenarios, namely scarce target-domain samples and completely unavailable target-domain data, domain adaptation and domain generalization methods for SAR target recognition are investigated, respectively. Furthermore, lightweight deployment and validation of the resulting models are realized on a domestically developed AI chip platform. Specifically, domain adaptation aims to reduce the distribution discrepancy between the source and target domains by exploiting a small amount of accessible target-domain data, whereas domain generalization focuses on mining domain-invariant information from the source domain to improve the generalization capability of the model on unseen target domains. The main research contents and contributions are summarized as follows.

(1) A domain adaptation method for SAR target recognition with scarce target-domain samples is proposed based on pixel-level domain alignment and class confusion loss. To address the problem that existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for SAR images rely excessively on large amounts of target-domain data, a contrastive learning-based unpaired image translation model is introduced at the pixel level to replace the conventional CycleGAN, and high-quality pixel-level domain alignment under limited-sample conditions is achieved through patch-wise noise contrastive estimation loss. At the feature level, a class confusion loss and a consistency regularization mechanism based on random data augmentation are designed. Through probability calibration and uncertainty weighting, the target-domain decision boundary is directly optimized, thereby reducing dependence on the complete statistical distribution of target-domain data. Results of unsupervised domain adaptation experiments on the SAMPLE dataset show that, in cross-domain SAR target recognition tasks, the proposed method can still maintain high recognition performance when target-domain samples are extremely scarce, indicating that it can effectively accomplish cross-domain alignment and decision-boundary optimization under few-sample conditions and substantially reduce the dependence of cross-domain SAR target recognition models on the quantity of target-domain data.

(2) A domain generalization method for SAR target recognition based on multi-level data augmentation and adversarial learning is proposed. For the extreme scenario in which target-domain data are completely unavailable, a domain generalization framework is constructed in which pixel-level data augmentation, feature-level style confusion, and domain adversarial learning operate collaboratively. In the pixel-level module, Gaussian noise injection, high-frequency frequency-domain perturbation, and decibel transformation are designed according to SAR imaging characteristics to enrich the diversity of source-domain data distributions. In the feature-level module, a domain style confusion mechanism based on instance normalization is developed to simulate diverse domain styles in deep feature space by mixing instance-level feature statistics. The domain adversarial learning module employs a gradient reversal layer to formulate a minimax game, thereby removing domain-specific information and learning domain-invariant features. Domain generalization experiments on the SAMPLE and S2M-5 datasets show that, in cross-domain SAR target recognition tasks, the proposed method achieves favorable recognition performance and cross-domain stability across different datasets, demonstrating that it can effectively improve model generalization and robustness on unknown measured domains when the target domain is unseen, and providing a practical strategy for mitigating performance degradation caused by unknown domain shifts in open-world settings.

(3) A lightweight deployment scheme for cross-domain SAR target recognition models on a domestically developed AI chip platform is implemented. Based on the two sets of ResNet50 weights containing cross-domain information obtained from the domain adaptation method in Chapter 2 and the domain generalization method in Chapter 3, a knowledge distillation framework with a ResNet50 teacher network and a MobileNetV2 student network is designed and validated. Through joint supervision with soft and hard labels, two types of cross-domain discriminative knowledge are transferred to a lightweight architecture, thereby effectively preserving cross-domain recognition capability while substantially reducing the number of model parameters. Based on the Huawei Ascend Model Compression Toolkit (AMCT), an INT8 post-training uniform quantization scheme is further devised. By adopting a hybrid strategy of per-channel symmetric quantization for weights and IFMR-based asymmetric calibration for activations, the model size is further compressed and inference latency is reduced. Using the Huawei Atlas 200I DK A2 developer kit, a complete deployment pipeline is established, covering model export, AMCT quantization, ATC conversion, and AscendCL-based on-board inference. Deployment experiments demonstrate that the proposed scheme can significantly compress model size and reduce storage overhead and inference latency while retaining favorable cross-domain recognition performance, thereby establishing a complete technical pipeline from cross-domain recognition model training to lightweight compression and deployment on domestically developed edge platforms.

\end{eabstract}
